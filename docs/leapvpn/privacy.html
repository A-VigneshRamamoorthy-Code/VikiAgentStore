Leap VPN — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 14 November 2026 · Developer: Vignesh Ramamoorthy (Dublin, Ireland)

Leap VPN is a client that sets up and controls a private VPN on infrastructure you own: virtual machines in your own Microsoft Azure subscription, or a PC in your home. The developer operates no VPN servers, cannot see your traffic, and receives none of your data.

What the developer collects

Nothing. The app has no analytics, advertising or crash-reporting SDKs, no accounts with us, and sends nothing to servers run by the developer.

Where your data goes

  • Your Azure subscription (Microsoft). When you sign in with your Microsoft account, the app uses Microsoft's sign-in (login.microsoftonline.com) and Azure Resource Manager (management.azure.com, storage.azure.com) to create, start and remove your own VPN servers. Sign-in tokens and WireGuard keys are stored in your device's Keychain. Your VPN traffic flows only between your devices and your own server. Microsoft's handling of your Azure account is covered by the Microsoft Privacy Statement.
  • Your VPN server. It runs in your subscription, keeps no traffic logs, and only you hold its keys. Its optional ad and tracker filter resolves DNS on that server; the blocklist is downloaded from a public list (oisd.nl). No browsing history is stored.
  • Location display. While connected, the app asks ipinfo.io for the public IP's country and city so it can show where you appear to be. Currency rates for cost estimates come from open.er-api.com, and Azure list prices from prices.azure.com. These requests carry no personal information beyond the IP address any web request reveals.
  • Purchases (Apple). Leap VPN Plus subscriptions are processed entirely by Apple. The developer receives no payment details. See Apple's Privacy Policy.

Children

The app is not directed at children and collects no data from anyone.

Deleting your data

Everything the app creates lives in your Azure subscription (resource groups whose names start with leapvpn) and on your device. Remove each location from Settings in the app, or delete those resource groups in the Azure portal, then delete the app.
