# Worked example — a pursuit

A live news chopper reports a high-speed chase. There is no chase.

```bash
S=../..                      # the 2d-animation style folder

python3 $S/scripts/compile.py beat-plan.json -o board.json \
        --motion-plan motion-plan.json
python3 $S/scripts/render.py board.json --sheet
python3 $S/scripts/render.py board.json -j 0                 # 77.7s, 1920x1080
python3 $S/scripts/compile.py beat-plan.json -o short.json \
        --motion-plan motion-plan.json --aspect 9:16         # the vertical cut
```

`pursuit_sheet.jpg` is the committed output of the second command. If your
sheet does not look like it, something in the style has changed.

## Narration is not committed

`vo/` holds sixteen clips generated by
[`voice-booth`](../../../voice-booth/) from [`lines.json`](lines.json) — 5 MB of
WAV that no repository needs, so it is ignored. Regenerate it before rendering.

Without it **the render does not fail**: it falls back to silence, and because
every shot is timed against the narration clock (`"at": "l8.end+0.3"`), the
whole film re-times shorter. If your runtime is not ~77.7 s, that is why.

## What to notice

- **The narrator is wrong, and that is the film.** She reports a pursuit at
  "speeds approaching twenty miles per hour" over pictures of a man walking
  home with a sandwich. Nothing in the pictures contradicts her directly; the
  audience does the work, which is the whole comic engine.
- **`SUSPECT VEHICLE` is captioned over a police car.** The chopper is
  circling the wrong vehicle. The caption is one word of set dressing doing a
  joke's worth of work.
- **The impact beat fills the frame.** `b14` is a red traffic light staged at
  `scale 3.1` so the lamp covers the screen. An `impact` gets no camera move by
  contract, so its accent has to come from the *cut* — and a cut only reads as
  an accent if the image genuinely changes. Staged small, against the same
  highway background as the shot before it, it measured as a quiet moment.
- **The sakuga beat is the only shot on ones.** `b13`, the helicopter, is the
  single most expensive shot in the film and it is 2.9 seconds long.
- **Five shots are under one second.** They are reaction cuts, and
  `render.py` warns about every one of them. The warning is right that this is
  faster than the style's 3–4 s average; the reference films cut like this
  during a gag, so they stay.
