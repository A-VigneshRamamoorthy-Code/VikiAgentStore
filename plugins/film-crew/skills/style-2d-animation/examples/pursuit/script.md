# Pursuit — shooting script

**Style:** `2d-animation` · **Runtime target:** ~95 s · **Aspect:** 16:9 (+ a 9:16 short)
**Narrator:** `imogen`, filtered `radio` — she is a diegetic reporter in a news
helicopter, not a voice-over.

---

## The engine of the joke

The reporter is describing a high-speed police pursuit. The pictures are
describing a man driving home.

Every laugh in this film comes from that single gap, and it widens on a fixed
schedule: she escalates, the picture deflates, and neither ever acknowledges the
other. Nobody in the film knows it is funny. **The moment a character winks at
the audience, the premise dies.**

This is also why the style earns its keep. A board style could show a car and a
helicopter. Only a rig can show a man *calmly eating a sandwich* while being
pursued, and the calmness is the joke.

## Structure

| act | lines | what turns |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — the report** | `l1`–`l4` | authority established, then the reveal undercuts it |
| **2 — the evidence** | `l5`–`l7` | she doubles down; he gets more polite |
| **3 — the standoff** | `l8`–`l11` | the fake climax — a red light |
| **4 — the arrival** | `l12`–`l16` | he parks, waves, and she has to say it out loud |

## Narration

Gaps carry the comedy. A gag's laugh lives in the **hold after it**, so the
punchline lines run long gaps and the setup lines run short ones.

| id | line | gap | on screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| `l1` | "Sky Nine is live above the eastbound ring road, where police are pursuing a suspect vehicle." | 0.5 | **Aerial.** Roads, rooftops, a police convoy. Genuinely dramatic. |
| `l2` | "We're told the driver has refused to stop for officers." | 0.5 | Chopper drifts. Chyron: `LIVE — POLICE PURSUIT`. |
| `l3` | "And there it is. The suspect vehicle." | **1.2** | **Reveal.** Push in on… a milk float. Hold on it. |
| `l4` | "Travelling at speeds approaching twenty-two miles per hour." | **1.1** | Street level. A cyclist overtakes him, unhurried. |
| `l5` | "The driver is showing no signs of panic whatsoever." | **1.0** | **Portrait.** Norman, both hands at ten-and-two, eating a sandwich. |
| `l6` | "He has indicated. Correctly. At every junction so far." | **1.2** | The indicator blinks. A police officer stares at it, defeated. |
| `l7` | "Police have now committed six vehicles to the operation." | 0.6 | Wide. An absurd convoy behind one milk float. |
| `l8` | "Wait — wait, he's making his move!" | **0.35** | **SAKUGA.** Whip-pan, chopper lurches, everything animated on ones. |
| `l9` | "He has stopped. A tense standoff at the junction of Wexley Road." | **1.3** | He has stopped at a red light. Hold. Let it sit. |
| `l10` | "Nobody is moving. Nobody dares." | **1.2** | Six police cars waiting politely. Someone's engine ticks. |
| `l11` | "The lights have changed." | 0.7 | Green. The entire convoy moves off in an orderly line. |
| `l12` | "He is indicating left. He is… parking." | **1.2** | He parks. Neatly. Within the lines. |
| `l13` | "The suspect has exited the vehicle. He is carrying what appears to be a sandwich." | **1.1** | Norman walks to his front door. Officers watch, unsure. |
| `l14` | "He is waving. At us." | **1.4** | He waves up at the chopper. **Straight to camera.** Hold. |
| `l15` | "…We may have been covering a man's drive home." | **1.5** | Aerial, pulling out slowly. The convoy just sits there. |
| `l16` | "Sky Nine. Back to the studio." | 1.6 | Chyron dies. Cut to black on the rotor. |

**≈152 words.** At Imogen's measured pace plus the gaps above, roughly **90–95
seconds**.

## Delivery notes for the voice booth

She is a professional having a bad day, not a comedian.

- `l1`–`l2` — full broadcast authority. Play it completely straight; the more
  credible she is here, the further she has to fall.
- `l4` — the hesitation before "twenty-two" is the whole line. She has read the
  number and does not believe it.
- `l8` — the only time she raises her voice. It has to be genuinely urgent, or
  the red light does not land.
- `l14` — flat. She has run out of report.
- `l15` — quiet, and slightly to herself. This is the admission.

## Motion budget

| tier | shots | where |
|---|---|---|
| `sakuga` | 1 | `l8` — the fake climax. The one cut that gets everything. |
| `impact` | 2 | `l3` reveal, `l9` the stop |
| `full` | ~4 | `l4`, `l7`, `l11`, `l13` |
| `limited` | ~5 | `l2`, `l5`, `l6`, `l12`, `l16` |
| `hold` | ~5 | `l9`, `l10`, `l14`, `l15` — the comic holds |

The distribution law in `animation-director` wants ≥35% `hold` and ≥62%
`hold`+`limited`. This film satisfies it *for story reasons* rather than
budgetary ones, which is the ideal case: the held shots are held because
stillness is funny here, and the one expensive cut is spent on a climax that
turns out to be a traffic light.

## Cast

| id | who | look |
|---|---|---|
| `norman` | the driver. Middle-aged, serene, sandwich. | cardigan; never hurried |
| `officer` | the nearest policeman. Increasingly demoralised. | uniform; does all the reacting |
| `cyclist` | overtakes on `l4`. One shot, no reaction. | the funniest thing in the film precisely because he ignores it |

## The vertical short

Cut `l3`–`l9` — reveal to red light. It is the tightest self-contained version
of the gag and needs no ending, because the standoff *is* a punchline. Re-shoot
in `9:16` rather than cropping; the convoy gag needs a re-stage, since six cars
in a horizontal line do not exist in a vertical frame. Stack them going away
from camera instead.

## Rights

Original fiction. No real broadcaster, force, place or person. "Sky Nine" and
"Wexley Road" are invented; the film makes no factual claim, so it carries no
research ledger and must never be packaged as though it did.
