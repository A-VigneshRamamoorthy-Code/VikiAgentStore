# The Summit

**Wordless.** No narration, no dialogue, no captions. Music, wind and a
handful of sound effects carry it — exactly as the reference films do.

Runtime target ~78 s. **Three cuts in the whole film** (2.3 / min), all on the
same locked-off wide composition. Nothing pans, nothing pushes. The mist does
the storytelling.

---

## The gag

A climber fights his way to the top of a remote peak in thick weather. He
plants himself on the apex, exhausted and triumphant. Then the mist thins —
and keeps thinning — and reveals a lamppost. Then a litter bin. Then a traffic
cone and a sign.

He is standing in a car park.

## Why it is built this way

The mist is doing three jobs at once, which is the whole reason the film works
at 78 seconds with a locked camera:

1. **It is the plot.** The reveal is the joke, and the reveal is just
   visibility increasing. No cut is needed to tell it.
2. **It is the frame-liveness mechanism.** The camera never moves and there
   are three cuts, so the drifting cloud and haze are the only thing keeping
   consecutive frames different — which is what the frozen-frame law
   (`longest_hold_s <= 2.5`) actually measures.
3. **It is the atmosphere.** Desaturated, high-value, soft-edged — the
   measured signature of the reference films.

## Shots

| # | time | mist | what happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00 – 0:22 | 1.00 | Whiteout. A hand, then a climber, hauls onto the apex. He stands, doubled over, breathing. Slowly straightens. Arms up. |
| 2 | 0:22 – 0:44 | 0.72 | He holds the pose, turning to take in a view that is not there. The murk lightens. Behind him, a vertical shape begins to resolve. |
| 3 | 0:44 – 1:02 | 0.38 | A lamppost. He notices it. Looks at it. Looks away. Looks back. |
| 4 | 1:02 – 1:18 | 0.05 | Clear. A bin, a cone, a sign. He lowers his arms and simply stands there. Hold on the drifting cloud. |

Four shots, three cuts. Summit cuts six times in 115 s; this cuts three times
in 78 s. Both sit inside the measured 0–4 cuts/min envelope.

## Sound

Wind throughout, thinning with the mist. Breathing on shot 1. One distant
birdsong as it clears. A single car door, far off, on the last beat — the only
sound that tells you where he is, arriving after the picture already has.
