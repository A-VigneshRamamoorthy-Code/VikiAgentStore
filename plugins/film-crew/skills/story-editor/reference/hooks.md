# Hooks — the first fifteen seconds

The opening does one job: convert a scroll into a commitment. Everything else in
the script is downstream of it.

---

## The word budget

At ~150 gross wpm you have **37–38 words** in fifteen seconds. At the
`voice-booth` default of `--rate="-15%"` you have closer to **28–32**.
Budget them before writing.

| Second | Beat | Words @150wpm | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | **Hook** | 7–8 | Trigger the orienting response. Open the governing loop. Pay the title/thumbnail promise. |
| 3–8 | **Stake** | 12–13 | Convert involuntary attention into curiosity. Name the consequence or the contradiction. |
| 8–15 | **Bridge** | 17–18 | Just enough context to orient — without closing the loop. Signal competence. End on escalation. |

---

## Hook taxonomy

| Hook | How it works | Example | Fails when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Curiosity gap** | Names a specific, closable gap in what the viewer knows | "There is one reason these fail, and it is not money." | The gap is vague, or the payoff is trivial |
| **In medias res** | Drops into the middle of the action, no context | "The door slammed. I had four minutes." | The viewer cannot orient within ~5s |
| **Negation** | Contradicts a belief the viewer holds | "Everything you know about this is backwards." | The audience never held the belief |
| **Stakes-first** | Declares what can be lost | "Get this wrong and it costs you five years." | The stakes are inflated beyond the content |
| **Question** | Poses a question the viewer needs answered | "Why does this street never go dark?" | The answer is obvious, or nobody wondered |
| **Bold claim** | A direct, falsifiable statement | "This ran for nine years without stopping." | Unsubstantiated past the first ten seconds |
| **Visual shock** | Breaks scroll autopilot with an image or sound | *glass shatters* "That is your savings." | Unrelated to the actual subject |
| **"You" hook** | Direct address makes it personal | "You have walked past this and never noticed." | The assumption does not fit the viewer |
| **List promise** | Promises a specific count | "Three sentences that change the answer." | One weak item makes the whole thing feel padded |
| **Flash-forward** | Shows the result, then rewinds | "This is how it ended. Now the part nobody saw." | The payoff is not striking enough to earn the rewind |
| **Villain framing** | Names a common antagonist | "The rule was written to make this impossible." | The enemy is not credible; reads as conspiracy |
| **Unfinished sentence** | Begins a thought and withholds the end | "The strangest part was—" | Overused, or the loop is never closed |

**Choosing one:** match the hook to the *shape of the content*, not to fashion.
A story with a genuine mystery takes the curiosity gap; a story with a person in
trouble takes in medias res; an argument takes negation.

## The click contract

The opening must pay the promise that earned the click. A viewer who arrives on
a specific expectation and meets a different subject leaves within seconds, and
the drop is attributed to your opening, not to the title.

- Acknowledge the promise within **eight seconds** — even a single phrase.
- The first frame should visually match the thumbnail.
- Never restate the title verbatim. Escalate it instead.

---

## Anti-patterns

Each of these costs measurable retention:

- **Logo or intro animation over ~2s.** Non-subscribers have no attachment to the
  brand; it is pure friction before value.
- **"Hey guys, welcome back."** Zero information density; signals the real content
  has not started, training viewers to skip.
- **Throat-clearing.** "So today I want to talk about…" — meta-commentary instead
  of content.
- **Setup before hook.** Context the viewer did not ask for. Curiosity first,
  context second.
- **Slow build.** Viewers who leave at second eight never reach the minute-three
  payoff. Front-load.
- **Subscribe request before value.** Breaks reciprocity; nothing has been earned.
- **Repeating the title.** The viewer already read it.
- **Apologising or disclaiming.** Frames the content as low-value before it starts.

**The reliable fix:** delete the first sentence of the first draft. In most
scripts it is the writer warming up, and the real hook is sentence two or three.

**The structural fact that matters most:** long-form YouTube rewards *absolute
watch time*; short-form rewards *completion rate*. A 15-minute video at 40%
retention beats an 8-minute video at 70%. Know which one you are optimising for
before choosing a length.

## Mechanisms, honestly labelled

- **Information-gap theory** (Loewenstein, 1994) — awareness of a *specific*
  missing piece produces a drive to close it. The best-supported mechanism here,
  and the reason vague teasing does not work.
- **Orienting response** — a pre-conscious reflex toward novelty, firing in under
  half a second. Buys 2–3 seconds of involuntary attention; the writing has to
  convert it.
- **Cognitive dissonance** — a confident contradiction of a held belief creates
  tension the viewer stays to resolve.
- **Parasocial trust** — competence and directness early determine whether the
  viewer grants you credit to delay the payoff.
- **Zeigarnik effect** — commonly cited for open loops. A 2025 meta-analysis found
  no general memory advantage for interrupted tasks. Do not lean on it; loops
  work because they are promises, not because of a memory trick.
