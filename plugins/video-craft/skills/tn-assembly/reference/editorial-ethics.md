# Editorial ethics

A channel covering a legislature has one asset: viewers believing the highlight
is actually a highlight. Every shortcut below spends that asset for a single
video's traffic.

## The confirmation rule

**Acoustic detection finds turmoil, not fights.** Applause, laughter,
desk-thumping, a procedural interruption where everyone talks at once, and an
audio fault all look like a shouting match on a spectrogram.

Before a title, thumbnail or Short hook claims a confrontation, **confirm it**
by watching or transcribing the segment. `clash_sigma` tells you how unusual the
moment was — treat a 4-sigma flag as worth checking and a 1.4-sigma one as
noise. It never tells you what happened.

## Do not manufacture drama

- **No invented clashes.** If the session was procedural, publish a digest and
  say so.
- **No misleading juxtaposition.** Two stills side by side imply an exchange
  between those two people. Take both from the *same* exchange, or do not use
  the split layout.
- **No hostile re-cutting.** Removing the answer from a question, or the
  qualifier from a claim, changes what was said. If a clip cannot stand with its
  reply attached, it is not a clip.
- **No fabricated quotes**, including in thumbnails. A phrase in a thumbnail
  reads as a quotation whether or not you put it in quotation marks.
- **No sound design that implies conflict** — no added crowd noise, no stings
  over a calm exchange.

## Attribution

Assembly webcasts are public records, but they still have a source. Name it in
every description, with a link. It costs one line and it is what separates a
channel from an aggregator.

Check the licence terms of the specific source before republishing. "Publicly
viewable" and "licensed for reuse" are different things.

## People

- **Politicians are fair game for their conduct in the chamber.** That is the
  point of a public record.
- **Do not caption someone's appearance, accent or health.**
- **Be careful with anyone who is not a public figure** — visitors, staff,
  officials in the gallery. They did not choose to be on camera. Prefer clips
  that do not centre on them.
- **A VIP's presence justifies the framing, never a claim about their views.**

## Where automation must stop

Nothing in this pipeline publishes on its own, and that is deliberate. Rendered
videos are left private for a human to approve.

A wrong title claiming a fight that turns out to be applause is not a bug you
can fix after the fact — the correction reaches a fraction of the people the
mistake did.

The stopping point is: **a human reads the title and watches the flagged moment
before it goes public.**

## Publish the quiet days

A channel that only appears when there is a fight is a channel viewers only
trust when there is a fight. A plain digest of what was decided has a lower
ceiling and builds the standing that the dramatic videos spend.

This is also why the pipeline is allowed to return **no** episodes. That output
is a legitimate result, not a failure to be tuned away.
